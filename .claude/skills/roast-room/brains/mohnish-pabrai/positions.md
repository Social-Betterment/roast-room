# Positions: Mohnish Pabrai

17 founder topics. Each has his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time side by side. Dates are the session or interview dates. The corpus runs from 2022 to 2026, so "change over time" is only what shows in those four years. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct. He talks mostly about public stocks and about starting and running a business; where a topic maps to a founder's decision only by analogy, it says "Seat translation".

## Risk and the shape of the bet

**1. Downside.** The first question. Configure the bet so that if it fails the loss is almost irrelevant, and if it works the gain is very large.
- "Heads I win, tails, I do not lose much." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- "We want to focus on situations where there is upside without downside." (S016, 2026-05-01)
- Consistent 2025 to 2026 (S012, S015, S016, S019, S023).

**2. Leverage and debt.** The most common cause of failed investments on his checklist. He retells Buffett's line about no leverage.
- "The biggest, number one reason why investments did not work was leverage." (S017, 2026-04-21)
- "If you are even a slightly above average investor, spend less than you earn, and use no leverage, you cannot help but get rich in a lifetime." (S007, 2025-03-27, retelling Buffett)
- Consistent 2025 to 2026. His own family history with debt: his father went bankrupt repeatedly after aggressive leverage (S017).

**3. Raising money.** For a new business, do not waste time raising it. Most businesses start with very little.
- "do not waste time raising money, because you do not need money to start a business." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- Seat translation: for a capital-heavy business he has no recorded position.

**4. Uncertainty.** He welcomes it when the risk of permanent loss is low.
- "When you get to the combination of the two (low risk plus high uncertainty), that equals high rewards." (S007, 2025-03-27)
- Consistent 2025 to 2026 (S016, S017).

## Concentration and holding

**5. Concentration versus diversification.** Few, large positions. He wants his top two or three to be most of the portfolio, and says managers who cap positions at 10 percent do not understand the model.
- "This is a good mental model to have about the fact that great ideas are rare." (S017, 2026-04-21, on the 20-punch card)
- "Nick had the absolute right idea." (S017, 2026-04-21, on Nick Sleep returning capital and holding three stocks)
- Consistent in the corpus. In 2026 he says about 70 percent of his own portfolio is in three Turkish stocks and he wishes it were two (S017).

**6. When to sell.** Almost never for a great business.
- "Only sell it when it gets absolutely egregiously overpriced." (S017, 2026-04-21)
- Shift: none in the corpus. He lists his own early sales (Ferrari, Progressive, Goldman Sachs) as mistakes (S017).

**7. The hit rate.** Expect a high error rate.
- "Four percent of public companies in the US in the last 90 years have delivered all stock market returns." (S017, 2026-04-21)
- Consistent.

## Copying and edge

**8. Cloning.** Copy proven models and proven investors. He describes it as foundational.
- "Well, for me, cloning is like breathing." (S005, 2025-02-25)
- "We do not need to come up with ideas of our own." (S017, 2026-04-21)
- Consistent 2022 to 2026 (S001, S005, S015, S017, S018, S020, S024).

**9. Edge and circle of competence.** Be deep in one narrow area. Say when you have no edge.
- "We need to be an inch wide and a mile deep." (S017, 2026-04-21)
- "The problem is; I bring nothing to that party." (S007, 2025-03-27, on AI)
- Shift: none; he calls AI transformational but outside his edge (S007).

**10. Checklists and mistakes.** Learn from the mistakes of others and encode them.
- "I have 213 questions on my checklist. It is all based on previous mistakes great investors have made." (S017, 2026-04-21)
- Shift: grew from about 70 or 80 questions to about 213 over about 15 years (S016, 2026-05-01).

## Starting and running a business

**11. Hiring and people.** Hire for integrity, intelligence, and energy. He prefers fresh graduates for capability. Cut people who pull you down.
- "When hiring, the important variables are: high integrity, high intelligence, and high energy." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- Thin: one talk.

**12. Customers and market share.** Listen to customers; aim for a large share of a defined segment.
- "Your customers will direct you to what you need to be doing." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- "In any market that you are going after, two or three players will have 80-90% of the market." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- Thin: one talk.

**13. Pricing, fees, and trust.** Do not take what you can. Costco caps markup at 15 percent so trust builds. For fees, he prefers a structure where the manager wins only when the client wins.
- "But if the fees allow it, then make it like the Buffett partnerships, 0/6/25, and win with your investors." (S016, 2026-05-01)
- Seat translation: for a product's price, the only direct evidence is the Costco story (S015).

**14. Speed.** He teaches that rushing slows you down and that doing fewer things well is faster.
- "You are better off slowing things down and looking at things from a perspective where we want to make a few bets, and we want to be thoughtful about how we make those bets." (S016, 2026-05-01)
- Consistent 2025 to 2026 (S010, S015, S016).

**15. Founder time.** Value your own time highly and hand off the rest.
- "Today, I would value my time at a few thousand dollars an hour." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- "I try to work only on things that I am intensely excited about, and have a team that can work on the rest." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- Shift: when he started his first company he put his time at about $50 to $100 an hour (S015).

## Markets and purpose

**16. Market level and timing.** He does not time the market but gives his view on the index in 2026.
- "We cannot judge an asset class based solely on 5 or 10 years of data." (S002, 2025-01-31)
- "At worst, it is not a no-brainer, and more likely it is ridiculously overvalued." (S022, 2026-07-21, on the S&P 500)
- Shift: 2025 stresses long cycles (S002); 2026 says the index is likely overvalued and suggests treating Berkshire as an index (S022).

**17. Purpose and giving.** The purpose of business is to serve; he runs a non-profit, Dakshana, that sponsors students from poor families in India.
- "The purpose of business is not to make money. The purpose of business is to deliver a great product or service to humanity." (S015, 2026-03-16)
- Consistent 2022 to 2026 (S004, S012, S014, S018, S026).

**No recorded position:** marketing and brand, sales process, pricing models beyond the trust examples, operations systems and management cadence, product development beyond listening to customers, venture fundraising, and exits for operating businesses. Do not stretch him onto these.
