# Frameworks: Mohnish Pabrai

17 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (shape the bet, size and count the bets, check before you commit, hold and avoid, start and run a business, live and give). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that use the framework or a clear synonym. Pabrai talks mostly about public stocks and about starting and running businesses; where this file turns a stock rule into a founder decision, it says "seat translation". Where he repeats a line from Buffett, Munger, or someone else, the quote is his retelling.

---

## Shape the bet

### 1. Heads I win, tails I do not lose much (Dhandho)
- **What it is:** Take bets where the loss is small if you are wrong and the gain is large if you are right. "Dhandho" is the Gujarati word for business; he reads it as a way of doing business where the downside is close to non-existent. He credits the phrase to others and treats it as his bedrock model.
- **Parts:** Start from the downside, not the upside. Keep the cost of being wrong small. Pick bets where doing nothing wrong is enough to win. Examples he gives: no-capital startups, the Patel motel owners, IPSCO (S001, S012, S015, S016).
- **When to use:** Any new bet, product, market, or hire. It is the first test.
- **How to score:** Rate each option 1 to 10 on payoff asymmetry: the best case divided by the worst case, in dollars and in months. A bet that can sink the business scores at most 3, however large the upside.
- **Quote:** "Heads I win, tails, I do not lose much." (S015)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S007, S010, S015, S016, S017, S021, S022)

### 2. Low risk, high uncertainty
- **What it is:** The market pays for certainty and punishes uncertainty. Look for businesses or bets where the risk of permanent loss is low but the outcome is hard for others to call. That mismatch is where the reward sits.
- **Parts:** Separate risk (chance of losing money for good) from uncertainty (not knowing the path). Wall Street loves low risk and low uncertainty and prices it to perfection. He wants the opposite (S007, S016, S017).
- **When to use:** Judging an option that looks scary because the future is unclear, but whose downside is protected (cash, contracts, assets).
- **How to score:** Rate the downside protection first (cash, contracted revenue, assets, low debt), then how unclear the outcome looks to others. High protection plus a murky outcome scores high. A murky outcome with no protection scores low.
- **Quote:** "When you get to the combination of the two (low risk plus high uncertainty), that equals high rewards." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S016, S017, S018)

### 3. Many small tries when the downside is not meaningful
- **What it is:** If failing costs little, try many things. Most will not work. The ones that work pay for many others. He points to Amazon's small bets and AWS as the model.
- **Parts:** Cap each try. Do not bet what you cannot lose. Let the winners fund the rest (S012, S015).
- **When to use:** Early-stage choices where the founder is unsure what will work and each test can be capped.
- **How to score:** Rate whether each test is capped and whether one win would cover many misses.
- **Quote:** "The ones that work, they work so much that it pays for another 50 of these side bets that do not work" (S012)
- **Sources:** 4 (S012, S015, S016, S023)

## Size and count the bets

### 4. Few bets, big bets, infrequent bets (the 20-punch card)
- **What it is:** Imagine you may buy only 20 stocks in a whole life. You would be extremely careful. Great ideas are rare, so make few bets, put real weight behind each, and wait a long time between them.
- **Parts:** The 20-punch card idea. One bet every three to five years over a lifetime. A litmus test for portfolio managers: do the top two or three positions add up to about 70 percent (S016, S017).
- **When to use:** Deciding how many projects, products, or markets to chase at once, and how much of the company's capital or time to put behind the best one.
- **How to score:** Rate the plan on focus. Many half-funded bets score low. One well-chosen, well-funded bet with a capped downside scores high.
- **Quote:** "In an investing lifetime of 70 or 80 years, on average, you would be making one bet every three to five years." (S016)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S005, S010, S016, S017, S022)

### 5. A high error rate is guaranteed
- **What it is:** Expect to be wrong often. A small share of companies delivers nearly all the returns, and even the best investors have a low hit rate. So the winners must be allowed to run and the misses must be survivable.
- **Parts:** Four percent of public companies produced all market returns. Buffett found about a dozen ideas in 60 years. Do not cut the flowers and water the weeds (S017).
- **When to use:** Judging a portfolio of bets, or a founder who needs every bet to work.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the plan survives a high miss rate. A plan that needs every bet to work scores low.
- **Quote:** "Four percent of public companies in the US in the last 90 years have delivered all stock market returns." (S017)
- **Sources:** 9 (S005, S008, S009, S010, S016, S017, S019, S023, S026)

## Check before you commit

### 6. The pre-investment checklist
- **What it is:** A list of questions built from the mistakes great investors have made. Run it before any commitment so the known blind spots show up first. His has 213 questions and grew from 70 or 80 over about 15 years.
- **Parts:** The three biggest buckets of failure are leverage, a misunderstanding of the moat, and a problem with leadership or ownership (S017, S024).
- **When to use:** Before committing money, time, or the company's focus. Seat translation: write the checklist from your own and others' past mistakes, and run it every time.
- **How to score:** Rate how many known failure modes have been checked. An unchecked leverage, moat, or leadership question caps the score at 5.
- **Quote:** "I have 213 questions on my checklist. It is all based on previous mistakes great investors have made." (S017)
- **Sources:** 7 (S010, S016, S017, S021, S022, S024, S026)

### 7. The 10-year-old test
- **What it is:** If you cannot explain the thesis to a 10-year-old in three or four sentences, do not invest. Simple theses leave fewer places for hidden risk.
- **Parts:** Three or four sentences. No spreadsheet. If it needs a model to justify, it is a pass (S016, S017).
- **When to use:** Any plan, deck, or pitch.
- **How to score:** Rate how plainly the plan can be said. A plan that only works inside a spreadsheet scores low.
- **Quote:** "If you cannot explain your investment thesis to a 10-year-old in 3 or 4 sentences, please do not invest." (S017)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S010, S016, S017, S020, S022)

### 8. Do the math in your head (no Excel)
- **What it is:** If the numbers have to be run through a model to work, the margin of safety is too thin. The right bets are clear on a napkin.
- **Parts:** Quick mental math. Ignore false precision. Trust a wide gap between price and value (S016, S017).
- **When to use:** Evaluating a price, a forecast, or a return claim.
- **How to score:** Rate the size of the gap between cost and value. A gap that needs three decimal places to see scores low.
- **Quote:** "If you cannot do the math in your head quickly, it is a pass." (S017)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S004, S005, S007, S008, S015, S016, S017, S020)

### 9. Circle of competence: an inch wide and a mile deep
- **What it is:** The size of your circle does not matter. What matters is being at its center and knowing it better than anyone else.
- **Parts:** Pick a narrow area. Learn the history and numbers cold. Stay inside it when you bet (S007, S017).
- **When to use:** Whether the founder has real knowledge in the area of the bet.
- **How to score:** Rate how deep the team is on this specific bet. Wide and shallow scores low. Narrow and deep scores high. He says plainly when something is outside his own (AI, S007).
- **Quote:** "We need to be an inch wide and a mile deep." (S017)
- **Sources:** 14 (S003, S004, S005, S007, S008, S009, S010, S016, S017, S018, S020, S022, S023, S024)

### 10. Shameless cloning
- **What it is:** Do not try to invent. Copy proven models, and copy the best. He says there is not one idea in his portfolio that he came up with. The world can fit several of the same thing.
- **Parts:** Study competitors and the best operators (Sam Walton visited more non-Walmart stores than anyone). Use public lists of what others bought (S005, S015, S017).
- **When to use:** Choosing a product, market, or approach. Ask who has already proven this, and whether a copy is possible.
- **How to score:** Rate how much of the plan is already proven by someone else. A plan with no proof and no clone scores low.
- **Quote:** "I recently looked at my entire portfolio. There is not one single idea in that portfolio that I came up with; not even one." (S017)
- **Sources:** 16 (S001, S004, S005, S007, S008, S009, S010, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S017, S018, S020, S026)

## Hold, and what to avoid

### 11. Circle the wagons: do not sell the great ones
- **What it is:** When you own a truly great business, keep it. Do not sell when it becomes fairly priced or overpriced; sell only at an absurd price. He counts his own early exits (Ferrari, Progressive, Goldman Sachs) as mistakes he regrets.
- **Parts:** Great businesses are found only a few times in a life. Keep them. Let them become a large share of the portfolio (S017).
- **When to use:** Deciding whether to sell, cut, or cash out of a winner.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the option sells a rare winner for a modest gain. Selling a rare winner early scores low.
- **Quote:** "Only sell it when it gets absolutely egregiously overpriced." (S017)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S008, S009, S010, S017, S020, S021, S023)

### 12. No leverage
- **What it is:** The most common reason investments failed in his checklist was leverage. Debt turns a bad stretch into a wipeout and forces a sale at the worst time.
- **Parts:** Do not buy levered businesses. Do not use leverage yourself. Retells Buffett's lunch line: spend less than you earn and use no leverage (S006, S007, S010, S017, S020).
- **When to use:** Any option that adds debt, fixed cost, or a personal guarantee.
- **How to score:** Rate the downside under a bad year with the debt in place. If the business cannot survive, the score is at most 2.
- **Quote:** "The biggest, number one reason why investments did not work was leverage." (S017)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S010, S011, S016, S017, S019, S020, S021, S024, S025)

### 13. Do not skim off the top
- **What it is:** Do not take fees or margin from the other side in a way that breaks trust. He favors the Buffett partnership structure (no fee, a hurdle, then a share of profits above it) and says he must charge a fee on his ETF only because he has to.
- **Parts:** Align your gain with theirs. Trust compounds. His example is Costco's 15 percent markup cap (S015, S016, S017).
- **When to use:** Pricing, fees, and incentive design. Seat translation: price so the customer wins too.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the price keeps the customer's trust over years. A price that grabs margin now but breaks trust scores low.
- **Quote:** "But if the fees allow it, then make it like the Buffett partnerships, 0/6/25, and win with your investors." (S016)
- **Sources:** 2 (S016, S017)

## Start and run a business

### 14. Start with no capital, keep the job, listen to customers
- **What it is:** A business can start for almost nothing. Do not waste time raising money. Keep your job and use the other 40 or 50 hours a week; switch only when it works. Show customers the idea and listen: they will tell you the pain point.
- **Parts:** No capital in, no downside. Reduce job effort to just above the firing line. A customer called him back to slide 10 and it became the whole deck (S015).
- **When to use:** A new venture, product line, or market test.
- **How to score:** Rate how little money and time are at risk before customers pay. A plan that needs funding before any customer evidence scores low.
- **Quote:** "The easiest way to eliminate risk when starting a business is not to quit your job, but to reduce your effort to just above the firing level." (S015)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S003, S015)

### 15. Take 70 percent, not 2 percent
- **What it is:** In any market two or three players hold most of it. "We only need 2 percent of a big market" is a sign the market was not segmented. Pick a segment you can dominate.
- **Parts:** Segment until your share can be large. Check who holds the market (S015).
- **When to use:** A market-size claim in a plan.
- **How to score:** Rate whether a leading share in a defined segment is realistic.
- **Quote:** "In any market that you are going after, two or three players will have 80-90% of the market." (S015)
- **Sources:** 8 (S006, S007, S010, S015, S017, S022, S023, S024)

## Live and give

### 16. The inner scorecard
- **What it is:** Measure yourself by your own standards, not by how others see you. It frees you to take unpopular bets and to ignore critics.
- **Parts:** Retells Buffett's question about the greatest lover who is known as the worst. Plus Teddy Roosevelt's man in the arena (S006, S019, S020).
- **When to use:** When the pull to look good is shaping the decision.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the option would still be chosen if no one were watching.
- **Quote:** "It is important to live by an inner scorecard." (S019)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S012, S014, S019, S020, S026)

### 17. Karam yogi: do the work, do not chase the reward
- **What it is:** From the Bhagavad Gita: do your duty and let go of the reward. He says that if you want to become wealthy, do not focus on wealth. Build something of value and the money follows.
- **Parts:** Focus on the work and the service. The purpose of business is to deliver a great product or service (S014, S015, S018).
- **When to use:** Founder motives. Seat translation: if the plan is built around the payout instead of the product, it is fragile.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the plan is anchored in a real product or service.
- **Quote:** "So, if you want to become wealthy, you cannot focus on being wealthy." (S014)
- **Sources:** 2 (S014, S018)
