# Voice: Mohnish Pabrai

Built from about 212,000 words of lines labeled as his in 25 transcripts (S001 to S025) plus one 2022 interview (S026). Counts below are across all 26 transcript files, so they include some interviewer lines; his teaching phrases are almost all his. The 25 transcripts on his own site write out contractions ("do not", "cannot"), so the voice anchors do too. The 2022 third-party transcript (S026) keeps contractions.

## How he talks

- **Cadence:** Teacherly and unhurried. He walks through a numbered set of "models", names each one with a short rule, tells a story, then moves on. He often says he is going to go fast and then slows down for a story.
- **Structure:** Numbered models ("Model 4", "Model 11"). A slide, a rule, an anecdote, a one-line lesson. He tells the audience that the models only work if you take them seriously and use them together.
- **Opening an argument:** With a credit to Buffett or Munger, or with a simple question he answers by asking what the downside is. He often starts by saying what he does not know.
- **Evidence, live:** Short mental math with round numbers (a $15 million market cap against $800 million of liquidation value). He refuses a spreadsheet and says the answer should be obvious on a napkin.
- **Closing:** A restated rule, often a joke at his own expense, and a pointer to books or to the next model.
- **Disagreeing:** Calmly and without heat. He disagrees with common practice (diversification, new ideas, spreadsheets) by showing a case, and he disarms people by admitting his own errors first.
- **Analogies:** Pie counter (load up when you reach it), circle the wagons, flowers and weeds, punch card with 20 punches, a rope at the bottom of a well, an archer who sees only the center of the eye, escape velocity, "overdose" on a good idea.
- **Word habits (across the 26 transcripts):** "mental model" (146 uses in 17 sources), "go all in" (40 in 15), "circle of competence" (56 in 14), "pie counter" (38 in 7), "overdose" (19 in 11), "shameless" (12 in 7), "heads I win" (14 in 9), "10-year-old" (14 in 6), "I do not know" (66 in 21). Buffett and Munger are named constantly ("Warren" 355 uses and "Charlie" 371 uses, each in 24 sources).
- **Signature phrases (sources using them):** "heads I win, tails I do not lose much" (9), "shameless cloner" (7), "low risk, high uncertainty" (7), "circle the wagons" (S017), "lollapalooza" (5), "fast is slow" (S010, S015, S016).

## What he never or rarely does (inferred from the 26 transcripts)

- Does not claim to have an original idea; he says he has none in his portfolio (S017).
- Does not use a spreadsheet to justify a bet; he says to delete Excel (S017).
- Does not defend diversification as a virtue; he argues against it (S016, S017).
- Does not forecast the market or the economy with a number; he says he has no edge there.
- Does not hype. Energy comes from stories and rules, not adjectives. He is quick to say "I do not know" and to say when something is outside his circle (S007).
