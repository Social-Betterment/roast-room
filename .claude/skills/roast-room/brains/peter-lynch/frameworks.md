Items tagged (K) come from the model's general knowledge of his public work, not from the 7 fetched sources; they have no quote and are lower confidence. Lynch speaks about public stocks. ## Know the business

### 1. Know what you own, and say why in a minute
- **What it is:** Before putting money in, you can explain what the business does and why you own it, in plain words, fast. "This one is going up" is not a reason.
- **Parts:** What it does. Why it will make more money. What would make you wrong. He sets the test at an 11-year-old or a fifth-grader and about a minute (S002, S003).
- **When to use:** Any decision that spends money, time, or the company's focus on something new, especially a product, market, or technology the founder does not work in.
- **How to score:** Rate each option 1 to 10 on how clearly the founder can state the plan and the reason, with no jargon. A plan that rests on "it will take off" scores low. Seat translation: the same test applied to the founder's own plan.
- **Quote:** "If you don’t understand what you own, you’re toast." (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005)

### 2. The edge: stay where you have an advantage
- **What it is:** People who work in an industry or live near a business see the change before the professionals. The edge is the thing you already know, so use it and do not throw it away.
- **Parts:** Your job. Your neighborhood and the places you shop. A supplier or customer relationship. He names chemical, insurance, steel, and shipping workers as examples (S003, S005).
- **When to use:** Choosing among markets or products; checking whether the founder is on home ground.
- **How to score:** Rate each option on how much of it sits inside what the founder and team already know firsthand. Far from the edge scores low unless the plan buys the edge cheaply (hire, partner, pilot).
- **Quote:** "I think the average person could know three or four or five companies very well." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S003, S005)

### 3. Kick the tires: check the idea from the shelf
- **What it is:** A hunch from daily life is the start of the work, not the end. He went to stores, bought the competing product, and had colleagues try it before he held a big position.
- **Parts:** Notice a product that works. Learn who buys it and how often. Check a competitor. Test it with others. Then look at the numbers (S001, S002).
- **When to use:** Any "I saw it working" story, including a founder's own enthusiasm for a product or channel.
- **How to score:** Rate each option on how many independent checks stand behind it beyond the founder's first impression. One anecdote and no checks scores low.
- **Quote:** "That’s what fundamental research is." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S005)

## Do the homework

### 4. The half-hour of work
- **What it is:** Most people research a refrigerator more than a stock. A short, real check beats none: does the thing have sales and profits, what does the balance sheet say, why do I own it.
- **Parts:** Sales and profits exist. Cash against debt. A written reason (S004, S005).
- **When to use:** Before any commitment of money. It is the minimum bar, not the whole job.
- **How to score:** Rate each option 1 to 10 on whether the basic numbers are on the page. Missing numbers cap the score at 4 until they are filled in.
- **Quote:** "A half an hour of work would save them a fortune." (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S003, S004, S005)

### 5. Turn over more rocks
- **What it is:** Good ideas come from volume of looking. Ten companies, one worth owning; twenty, two; forty, four. Look at unfashionable places too.
- **Parts:** Widen the search. Look in dead industries, companies with unions, small and obscure names. Do the work on each (S001, S002, S003).
- **When to use:** When a founder has fallen for the first or only option; when the option list is narrow.
- **How to score:** Rate how many real alternatives were examined. One option studied scores low. Seat translation: add the missing alternatives to the brief.
- **Quote:** "The person that turns over the most rocks wins the game." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S003)

### 6. Paper portfolio and written reasons
- **What it is:** To learn whether you are any good at this, pick ten stocks on paper, write three or four bullets on why for each, and check the facts three months later.
- **Parts:** Ten picks, short bullets, a review of what happened to the facts (S002, S005).
- **When to use:** A founder asking whether they can judge a market or a hire; a cheap way to test judgment before betting. Seat translation: write the reasons down before the test and score them afterward.
- **How to score:** Rate each option on whether the reasons are written and checkable. Unwritten reasons score low.
- **Quote:** "Put together a $100,000 paper portfolio of at least 10 stocks to see if you're good at this." (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S005)

### 7. Balance sheet first when the news is bad
- **What it is:** Two losing businesses are not equal. The one with cash and no debt can wait; the one with heavy debt may not survive the wait.
- **Parts:** Left side, cash. Right side, debt. Will the company be around (S004, S005).
- **When to use:** Any option that adds fixed cost or debt, or any turnaround story.
- **How to score:** Rate the downside by cash cover against the plan's costs. Debt that can sink the company when the story is slow scores very low.
- **Quote:** "You want to look to see is this company gonna be around?" (S004)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S005)

## Read the story

### 8. Know the story, and ask what inning it is
- **What it is:** Every investment has a story: why earnings will grow. Follow it. When the story is still early, you have time. When it is spent, it is time to leave. He does not buy and forget.
- **Parts:** The story in a sentence. The room left to grow (Walmart in 18 percent of the country, McDonald's overseas). What would end it (Toys R Us, Kodak, Xerox) (S001, S004, S005).
- **When to use:** Any growth bet, channel, or market entry. Ask how much runway is left and what shows the story is changing.
- **How to score:** Rate each option on room to grow, evidence the story is working now, and a named signal that it would stop. No signal scores low.
- **Quote:** "You have to make sure you're following the story. You don't just buy it and forget about it." (S005)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S005)

### 9. Facts over forecasts
- **What it is:** He does not predict the economy or the market. He looks at facts he can check now: used car prices for auto stocks, aluminum inventories for aluminum, how many hotels are being built for hotels.
- **Parts:** Name the few facts that drive this business. Watch them. Skip the forecast (S001, S002, S003, S004).
- **When to use:** When the case for an option leans on a prediction about the economy, rates, or the market.
- **How to score:** Mark down any option whose payoff depends on a macro call. Rate up options that work across several outcomes.
- **Quote:** "I deal in facts, not forecasting the future." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S003, S004)

### 10. Six kinds of company (K)
- **What it is:** (K) A way to say what you are buying: slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, turnarounds, and asset plays. Each is judged by a different test and has a different job in a portfolio. Not in the fetched sources, which mention turnarounds and cyclicals only in passing (S003, S004).
- **When to use:** Seat translation: name which kind of move this is (steady core, fast growth, cycle play, rescue) before judging it.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the plan's expectations match the kind. A fast-growth hope on a slow-grower business scores low.
- **Quote:** None (recalled, not verbatim).
- **Sources:** 0 fetched (K)

## Hold and size

### 11. Winners run, and let the big ones cover the mistakes
- **What it is:** Selling winners and keeping losers is like cutting flowers and watering weeds. You do not need to be right most of the time; a few big winners pay for the misses.
- **Parts:** Right about six in ten. Hold the winners, add when the story improves. A stock you own can only fall to zero, but it can rise tenfold (S001, S002, S003, S004).
- **When to use:** Deciding whether to cut a project, add to a winner, or judge a portfolio of bets.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the downside is capped and the upside is open. A bet with the reverse shape scores low.
- **Quote:** "You want to let the winners run." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S003, S004)

### 12. The stomach test and the time horizon
- **What it is:** Anyone can pick stocks; the hard part is surviving the declines. Money you need in a year or two does not belong in stocks.
- **Parts:** Can you sleep through a 10 percent drop. When do you need the cash. Declines come about every two years (S001, S003, S005).
- **When to use:** Any bet that uses cash the founder will need soon (payroll, a tuition-style deadline, runway).
- **How to score:** Rate each option on whether a bad stretch would force a sale at the worst time. Forced selling scores low.
- **Quote:** "stomach is the key organ here. It’s not the brain." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S003, S005)

### 13. Price against earnings, and PEG (K)
- **What it is:** (K) A stock's price should be judged against its earnings and how fast they grow; the PEG ratio compares the price to earnings to the growth rate. The sources show the first half: over time the stock follows earnings (S004), and a very high price to earnings draws a caution (S002).
- **When to use:** Seat translation: when a founder asks what a growth bet is worth, ask what earnings or cash it must produce and by when.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the expected cash result justifies the cost. Pay-off with no number scores low.
- **Quote:** "It’s magic, that in 20 years, the Dow Jones Average has tripled and the profits of the Dow Jones companies have tripled." (S004)
- **Sources:** 2 fetched (S002, S004), plus (K)

### 14. Do not diworsify (K)
- **What it is:** (K) Buying or building more and more unrelated things is a way to wreck a good business. Stay with what you understand and have shown you can run. The fetched sources point the same way: follow five or six companies, own one or two (S003).
- **When to use:** Seat translation: a founder adding a new line or market that is far from the core.
- **How to score:** Rate each option on how close it is to what the business already does well.
- **Quote:** None (recalled, not verbatim).
- **Sources:** 1 fetched (S003), plus (K)
