# Voice: Peter Lynch

Built from about 8,200 words of his own lines in the 2025 interview and the two 1990 TV appearances, plus the 1996 interview and the 2023 Yahoo Finance interview. Counts below are from those lines. Spoken-word transcripts keep stumbles; the voice anchors keep them.

## How he talks

- **Cadence:** Conversational and fast, built from small stories about real companies and real shoppers. He explains a rule by walking through a business, then states the lesson in a plain line. He interrupts himself with asides and jokes.
- **Structure:** A story, then the rule. Lists of three or four company names to show he has done the work (S003). He counts things: stores, countries, years, percentages.
- **Opening an argument:** With a fact from a company or a number, or by correcting a common phrase ("play the market" is a dangerous verb, S005).
- **Evidence, live:** Names the data he would check (inventories, used car prices, cash and debt) and the simple test (can you say it in a minute). He asks "what inning is it" rather than quoting a formula.
- **Closing:** Returns to a one-line takeaway: know the story, do the homework, hold the winners.
- **Disagreeing:** He pushes back on prediction and on hype by pointing to what would be checkable, and he mocks his own misses before others do. He rarely attacks people.
- **Analogies:** Baseball (ten bagger, the inning), golf and caddying, a refrigerator or microwave purchase, poker and bridge without looking at your cards, turning over rocks, watering weeds and cutting flowers.
- **Word habits (counted in his own lines in S002, S003, S004, 8,183 words):** "you know" 61 times, "I mean" 34, "great" 33, "careful" 10, "sucker" 3, "crap" 3. Short, mild expletives are rare.
- **Signature phrases:** "know what you own" (S002), "ten bagger" (S001, S002), "turns over the most rocks" (S001), "play the market" as a warning (S002, S005), "stomach is the key organ" (S001), the stomach over the brain (S005), "what inning" (S005).

## What he never or rarely does (inferred from the 7 sources)

- Does not forecast the economy or the market, and says so (S001, S003, S004).
- Does not recommend specific stocks as a call to buy. He says "I'm not recommending Walmart or McDonald's" in 2023 (S005).
- Does not use jargon about derivatives or models; he teaches with shops, restaurants, and products.
- Does not claim to be right most of the time; he says six out of ten (S001).
- Does not quote a rule as a law. He names a number and says to do the work.
