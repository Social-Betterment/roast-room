# Positions: Peter Lynch

16 founder topics. Each has his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time side by side. Dates are publish or air dates. This corpus is thin (7 sources, 1990 to 2025) and Lynch talks about public stocks, not private companies, so most founder topics map only by analogy; those say "Seat translation". "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct. Items tagged (K) are recalled, not sourced.

## Knowledge and homework

**1. Understanding the thing you put money in.** The most important rule. If you cannot say what it does and why you own it, stay out or buy a fund.
- "I have this expression, know what you own." (S002, 2025-10-06)
- "If you can’t do that, you should buy a fund." (S002, 2025-10-06)
- Consistent 1990 to 2025.

**2. Technology and things he does not understand.** He avoided tech he could not follow and still joked about it in 2025. He says he sometimes bought tech near the high, and that some tech names are really cyclicals.
- "I am the lowest tech guy ever." (S002, 2025-10-06)
- "I have zero AI stocks" (S002, 2025-10-06)
- Shift: none in stance. 1990 says he avoids technology because he does not understand what it does (S003, 1990-01-05); 2025 says the same about AI (S002). Apple is his stated miss: it was simple enough to understand and he did not look (S005, 2023-06-20).

**3. Doing the work.** Short, real homework beats none; people research small purchases more than big ones.
- "A half an hour of work would save them a fortune." (S004, 1990-11-16)
- "This is not like doing your taxes." (S005, 2023-06-20)
- Consistent 1990 to 2023.

## Forecasting and the market

**4. Predicting the economy or the market.** Futile. He deals in facts he can see now.
- "I deal in facts, not forecasting the future." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- "I’ve always said if you spend 13 minutes a year on economics, you’ve wasted 10 minutes." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- Consistent 1990 to 2025. He told the 1990 audience Fidelity had no economists (S003).

**5. Market timing.** Waste of effort; stay in, and add on declines if the money is not needed.
- "Time is on your side in the stock market." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- Shift: none. S001 gives a 30-year test of picking the best and worst day each year, with returns close together; see stories.

**6. Valuation of the whole market.** In 1990 he argued stocks follow earnings and are "always fairly valued", with price tied to profit over time. In 2025 he flags a very high price to earnings on a great company.
- "They’re always fairly valued." (S004, 1990-11-16)
- Shift: 1990 stresses the earnings link and says to buy great companies in declines (S004); 2025 calls Costco's high multiple "Ooph" while still calling it a great company (S002, 2025-10-06).

## Risk and sizing

**7. How many things to follow and own.** A person should follow a handful and own one or two.
- "I think a person should only follow five or six companies." (S003, 1990-01-05)
- Shift: none.

**8. Winners, losers, and being wrong.** Let winners run, add when the story improves, accept that you will be wrong often.
- "You want to let the winners run." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- "In this business if you’re good, you’re right six times out of ten." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- Shift: 1997 says right six of ten; 2023 says six, maybe six and a half (S005); 2025 says right "six and a half times out of 10" is a great score (S002).

**9. Volatility and the stomach.** Declines are normal; know your time horizon and do not hold money you need soon.
- "If you’re not ready for that, you shouldn’t be in the stock market." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- "If you’re getting up at 3:00 AM and looking things over, you shouldn’t be investing." (S005, 2023-06-20)
- Consistent 1990 to 2025.

**10. Debt and the balance sheet.** Cash against debt separates two losing companies.
- "If you can’t understand the balance sheet, go to some other stock." (S005, 2023-06-20)
- Consistent where mentioned (S004, S005).

## Growth and change

**11. Stage of a growth story.** Ask what inning it is. A business can be 25 years old and still early if it is in a fraction of its market.
- "You have to ask: what’s the inning of the ball game?" (S005, 2023-06-20)
- Consistent 1990 to 2023 (Walmart in S004, S001, S005).

**12. Turnarounds.** Buy when things move from terrible to less terrible, if the company will be around. Check solvency.
- "You want to look to see is this company gonna be around?" (S004, 1990-11-16)
- Consistent 1990 to 2025 (S003, S004, S005, S002).

**13. When a story ends.** Good stories can go bad; keep following them.
- "You have to make sure you’re following the story." (S005, 2023-06-20)
- Consistent.

## People and the company

**14. Management quality.** He singles out founders and CEOs who built businesses from small beginnings, and a manager at Fidelity who worked harder and more carefully than he did. This is a thin and indirect position.
- "I mean those are great people." (S002, 2025-10-06)
- Seat translation: judge the leader as a fact in the story, not a personality.

**15. Founder time and burnout.** He retired from Magellan at 46 because of hours and family, not pressure.
- "It wasn’t the pressure." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- "I was working six days a week and that wasn’t even enough." (S001, 1997-01-14)
- Shift: none; 2025 says his father died at 46 and he wanted more time with his family (S002).

## Other topics

**16. Hype and speculation.** New ways to gamble keep appearing; more ways to gamble means more ways to lose.
- "There’s more ways to gamble now than 35 years ago." (S005, 2023-06-20)
- Shift: 1990 warns against options and "playing the market" (S003, S004); 2023 adds crypto, meme stocks, sports betting (S005).

**No recorded position:** pricing, hiring and compensation, raising venture capital, partnerships, pivots, brand, and sales process. He talks about management and competition only as they affect a public company's profits (S001, S002). Do not stretch him onto these.
