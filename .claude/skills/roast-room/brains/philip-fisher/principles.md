# Principles: Philip Fisher

18 decision rules, clustered across sources. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Quotes are verbatim. S003 to S008 are second-hand (a blog or column quoting him or summarising a book), so a rule that rests only on them says so. Fisher talks about public stocks; the seat translates each rule to a founder's decision and says when that is a stretch.

## People and management

**1. Integrity is a pass-fail gate. If it is in doubt, nothing else matters.**
- "Does the company have a management of unquestionable integrity?" (S004)
- "genuine sense of trusteeship for the stockholders, sooner or later the stockholders may fail to receive a significant part of what is justly due to them." (S008)
- Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

**2. Live with a management before you trust it, and listen for conviction.**
- "Getting to know the management of a company is like getting married." (S001)
- "I have to figure out whether they're answering with real conviction." (S002)
- Sources: 2 (S001, S002). Confidence: medium.

**3. Ask the people around the business. The pitch tells you what they guess you want to hear.**
- "Managements will try to answer my questions by making a fairly shrewd guess at what I want to hear." (S002)
- "The only way I know is to stick my long nose in and ask questions." (S002)
- Sources: 3 (S002, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

**4. Prefer teams that keep their people and let them disagree.**
- "I prefer companies that have taken the route of holding on to their people by working for growth so there are enough legitimate jobs to go around." (S002)
- "I want companies that welcome dissent, rather than stifle it, that don't penalize people who criticize what management is doing." (S002)
- Sources: 3 (S002, S004, S007). Confidence: high (S004 and S007 give the personnel-relations point second-hand).

## The business and its growth

**5. Buy the best business you can find at a price that is not silly, not the cheapest thing on the screen.**
- "Then there is my approach, which is to find something so good" (S001)
- "The disadvantage of Ben Graham's approach, as he preached it, is it is such a good method that practically everybody knows it" (S001)
- Sources: 4 (S001, S005, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

**6. Look for a long runway and a reason rivals cannot share it.**
- "Buy into companies that have disciplined plans for achieving dramatic long-range growth in profits and that have inherent qualities making it difficult for newcomers to share in that growth." (S004)
- "They are all low-cost producers; they are all either world leaders in their fields or can fully measure up to another of my yardsticks, the Japanese competition." (S001)
- Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

**7. Do not read growth from research spending alone, and do not read a flat stretch as a stall without asking why.**
- "Actually, when bringing on new products, R&D, while important, is less costly than the combination of marketing money, when you're first introducing those new products, and the high-cost production when you first start to get those new products out" (S001)
- Sources: 2 (S001, S005). Confidence: medium.

**8. Look at tomorrow's story, not yesterday's.**
- "The things most companies boast about are yesterday's story." (S002)
- "I want to know: Who is working on the things that others are barely aware of?" (S002)
- Sources: 1 (S002). Confidence: low.

**9. A high multiple does not by itself mean the growth is already priced in.**
- Second-hand: Fisher's fictitious company that trades at twice the market multiple for thirty years and has no reason to change (S006). No verbatim line.
- Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low.

## Holding and selling

**10. If the job was done right at the start, the time to sell is almost never. Price alone is not a reason.**
- "If you are in the right companies, the potential rise can be so enormous that everything else is secondary." (S003)
- "If one of my stocks gets overpriced, I warn my clients that things may be unpleasant for a little while but it will rise to a new peak later." (S003)
- "if the job has been correctly done when a common stock is purchased" (S006)
- Sources: 4 (S001, S003, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

**11. Sell only when the business has changed, the buy was a mistake, or a clearly better use exists.**
- "If I think management or the basic situation has deteriorated, I will sell." (S003)
- Sources: 3 (S003, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

**12. Give a strong conviction about three years to show up.**
- "If I have a deep conviction about a stock but it has not performed after three years, I will sell it." (S003)
- Sources: 2 (S003, S008). Confidence: medium.

**13. Do not chase many small wins. Wait for the few very large ones.**
- "I don't want to spend my time trying to earn a lot of little profits. I want very, very big profits that I'm ready to wait for." (S001)
- "It is just appalling the nerve strain people put themselves under trying to buy something today and sell it tomorrow." (S001)
- Sources: 2 (S001, S003). Confidence: medium.

**14. Take losses early and small; never hold only to get back to even.**
- "Either this is a better investment than another one or a worse one." (S001)
- "more money has probably been lost by investors holding a stock that they really did not want until they could 'at least come out even' than from any other single reason." (S006)
- Sources: 3 (S001, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

## Risk, timing, and focus

**15. Concentrate in a few things you understand.**
- "For individuals (in possible contrast to institutions and certain types of funds), any holding of over twenty different stocks is a sign of financial incompetence. Ten or twelve is usually a better number." (S008)
- "I have four core stocks that are exactly the thing I want." (S001)
- Sources: 4 (S001, S003, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

**16. Do not try to time it. When you are not sure, hold some cash.**
- "Timing these things is so damnably difficult." (S001)
- "When you're not sure, you hedge." (S001)
- Sources: 2 (S001, S005). Confidence: medium (S005 says he came out against timing; second-hand).

**17. Stay away from heavy debt, even in good companies.**
- "And I don't like highly leveraged companies, even if they are well run." (S002)
- Second-hand: fixed interest payments greatly raise the risk of owning the stock (S007).
- Sources: 2 (S002, S007). Confidence: medium.

**18. Be wary of what the crowd is rushing into, but do not be a contrarian for its own sake.**
- "If there's standing room only, that's usually a pretty fair sign it's not a good time to buy the stock." (S001)
- "Part of real success is not being a 100% contrarian." (S001)
- Sources: 1 (S001). Confidence: low.
