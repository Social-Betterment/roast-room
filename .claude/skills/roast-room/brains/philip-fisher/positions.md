# Positions: Philip Fisher

16 founder topics. Each has the stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time side by side. Dates are publish dates. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct. The corpus is very thin: one long 1987 interview, one 1996 Q&A, and second-hand summaries of his books (S003 to S008). Fisher speaks about public companies; the stance on a founder's topic is the seat's translation and is marked when it is one.

## People and management

**1. Hiring and picking a leader.** Judge integrity and ability separately, and judge them by living with the team or asking people around it. Translation: the same test applies to a senior hire or a partner.
- "Getting to know the management of a company is like getting married." (S001, 1987-10-19)
- "Managements will try to answer my questions by making a fairly shrewd guess at what I want to hear." (S002, 1996)
- Shift: Consistent 1987 to 1996 on judging people by conviction and track.

**2. Layoffs and treatment of staff.** Against firing people to flatter earnings while top pay rises. Prefers keeping staff by growing.
- "Companies that are firing people right and left to make a good showing on their earnings statements while two or three top people get big boosts in their income." (S002, 1996)
- Shift: Only one date. No recorded position in 1987.

**3. Culture and dissent.** Wants an openly critical culture.
- "I want companies that welcome dissent, rather than stifle it, that don't penalize people who criticize what management is doing." (S002, 1996)

**4. Honesty with owners.** Flat standard; management that talks freely only in good times is a warning sign.
- "Does the management talk freely to investors about its affairs when things are going well but" (S004, undated)
- Shift: Consistent in the book summaries (S004, S005, S008).

## Growth and products

**5. New products and R&D.** Do not equate growth with research spending. Launch years cost more in marketing and early production and can flatten earnings.
- "Now people who are interested in growth but who aren't very sophisticated tend to measure it by how much you spend on R&D." (S001, 1987-10-19)
- Shift: 1987 talks about quality control and planning; 1996 says those are table stakes and asks who is working on things others barely see (S002).

**6. Sales and marketing.** Wants an above-average sales organization and a team that supplies what customers need today, not yesterday (S004, S007, second-hand). No verbatim line.
- "Does the company have an above-average sales organization?" (S004, undated)

**7. Margins and cost.** Wants a worthwhile profit margin, a plan to keep it, and to be a low-cost producer.
- "They are all low-cost producers; they are all either world leaders in their fields or can fully measure up to another of my yardsticks, the Japanese competition." (S001, 1987-10-19)

**8. Competition and moats.** The business must have qualities that make it hard for newcomers to share in the growth.
- "Buy into companies that have disciplined plans for achieving dramatic long-range growth in profits and that have inherent qualities making it difficult for newcomers to share in that growth." (S004, undated)

## Money, risk, and focus

**9. Raising money and dilution.** Asks whether growth will need so much new equity that the extra shares cancel the benefit to existing holders (S004).
- "In the foreseeable future will the growth of the company require sufficient equity financing so that the larger number of shares then outstanding will largely cancel the existing stockholders" (S004, undated)
- No recorded position on how a founder should raise a round.

**10. Debt.** Does not like heavy debt, even in well-run companies.
- "And I don't like highly leveraged companies, even if they are well run." (S002, 1996)

**11. Focus and number of bets.** A few big positions.
- "I have four core stocks that are exactly the thing I want." (S001, 1987-10-19)
- Shift: The 1998 columns report a limit of twenty holdings and a preference for ten or twelve (S006, S008, second-hand).

**12. Cash and uncertainty.** Holds some cash when unsure.
- "When you're not sure, you hedge." (S001, 1987-10-19)

**13. Timing the market and the economy.** Does not claim to time it, yet gave a macro view in 1987.
- "I haven't the faintest idea whether we are in 1927 or 1929." (S001, 1987-10-19)
- Shift: In 1987 he expected hyperinflation within a couple of years (S001). He did not give one in the 1996 excerpt. His record on that call is outside the corpus.

## Holding, exits, and mistakes

**14. When to sell or stop.** Almost never for price. Only for a mistake, a change in the business, or a better use.
- "If I think management or the basic situation has deteriorated, I will sell." (S003, 2020-01-17, quoting Forbes 1996)
- Shift: Same message in 1987 (hold the winners 8 or 9 to 30 years, S001) and 1996.

**15. Mistakes and losses.** Part of the cost of being in business. Cut small ones fast.
- "That's almost the cost of being in business." (S001, 1987-10-19)

**16. Dividends.** A minor point for a growth investor; growth companies grow their payouts more over time (S006, second-hand). No verbatim line.

**No recorded position:** pricing, fundraising mechanics, hiring process, marketing channels, customer acquisition, operations, software or AI strategy (he says in 1996 he is now interested in how a company uses computer hardware and software, S002), and anything about private companies.
