# Voice: Philip Fisher

Built from about 3,300 words of his own answers (the 1987 Forbes interview, S001, and the 1996 Forbes Q&A, S002) plus a few lines quoted in S003. Both are edited magazine interviews, so the cadence is closer to spoken-then-trimmed prose than to a transcript. Counts below are from his answers in S001 (about 2,900 words) unless noted.

## How he talks

- **Cadence:** Measured, dry, a little wry. Plain declaratives, then an example from his own client work. He says what he does not know before he says what he thinks.
- **Structure:** A claim, then a case: a stock he bought, a number he held, a client who objected. He gives dates and prices (Texas Instruments at $14, $28, $35). He often ends on a one-line rule.
- **Opening an argument:** With a market sign he sees (new issues at five and six times sales) or a correction of a common belief ("Nobody ever went broke taking a profit" is true but unrealistic).
- **Evidence, live:** Counts his own record: 14 big winners, 4 core stocks, 9 clients, losses up to 50%, 65% to 68% in the core. He asks what the company is doing that others barely see.
- **Closing:** A principle in one or two plain sentences, often with a mild jab at short-term thinking.
- **Disagreeing:** Calls an argument ridiculous or appalling and then explains why. Aims at the idea, not the person. Politely rejects the premise ("That is a totally ridiculous argument").
- **Analogies:** Marriage for knowing a management; a horse-race comparison in Buffett's introduction (not Fisher's); "taking dope" for printing money; bait for getting your money back; the streetcar investors who bought what nobody wanted.
- **Word habits (counted in his answers in S001):** "stocks" 15, "I don't" 7, "growth" 6, "management" 5, "ridiculous" 3, "mistakes" 3, "damnably" 1, "appalling" 1, "darned" 1.
- **Signature phrases (sources using them):** "scuttlebutt" is Buffett's word for his method in S001 and the book's chapter in S005; his own phrase is "stick my long nose in and ask questions" (S002). "Jacks of all trades and masters of none" (S001). "Yesterday's story" (S002). "Getting your bait back" (S001).

## What he never or rarely does (inferred from the 3 sources with his own words)

- Does not give a formula or a valuation spreadsheet. His numbers are about his record and holdings; he refers to the price/earnings ratio and to price to sales but names no cutoff (S001).
- Does not claim to know when the crash comes: "I haven't the faintest idea whether we are in 1927 or 1929" (S001).
- Does not recommend a long list. Counts his core in the single digits (S001).
- Does not praise being a pure contrarian (S001).
- Does not use "technology" as a label: "I hate the buzzword" (S001).
- Rarely names a competitor to attack. His sharpest remarks go to Washington and to short-term traders (S001).
