# Frameworks: Howard Marks

16 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (think, price, risk, cycles and psychology, timing and humility). "Sources" counts distinct memos in the corpus that use the framework or a clear synonym. Marks writes about markets and credit; where this file turns an investing idea into a decision for a founder, it says "seat translation". Where he is repeating a line from someone else (Buffett, Keynes, Dimson, and so on), the quote is his retelling.

## Think

### 1. Second-level thinking
- **What it is:** To beat the average you must think differently and better than the average. First-level thinking is an obvious opinion; second-level thinking asks what the range of outcomes is, what the consensus believes, and how your view differs and why you are right.
- **Parts:** The range of likely outcomes. Which one you expect. The probability you are right. What the consensus thinks and how yours differs. What the price already assumes (S099, S139).
- **When to use:** Any decision where "everyone knows" the answer is the starting point. Ask what is already priced in.
- **How to score:** Rate each option on how far the case rises above the obvious view. An option that only works if the consensus is right scores low. An option backed by a clear, reasoned difference from consensus scores high.
- **Quote:** "In order to outperform, your thinking has to be different and better." (S139)
- **Sources:** 7 (S099, S100, S117, S128, S139, S144, S160)

### 2. The things everyone knows
- **What it is:** What everyone knows is already in the price, so it cannot be the basis of an edge. Look for what the crowd has wrong or has not considered.
- **Parts:** Ask what the consensus is. Ask what would have to be true for it to be wrong. Weigh the cost of being early (S099, S141).
- **When to use:** Reading a popular trend, a hot market, or a plan that rests on a widely held belief.
- **How to score:** Rate the gap between the plan's premise and what is commonly believed. No gap, no edge.
- **Quote:** "Almost every tool an investor might employ is a two-edged sword." (S099)
- **Sources:** 39 (S005, S012, S015, S021, S026, S028, S030, S043, S045, S050, S052, S053, and 27 more)

## Price and value

### 3. The relationship between price and value
- **What it is:** Quality alone does not make a good investment. What matters is the price paid against the value received. A great asset can be a bad purchase; a poor one can be a good purchase at the right price.
- **Parts:** Know the value. Know the price. Buy when the gap favors you (S012, S032, S075).
- **When to use:** Any decision that spends money for a stake in something: an acquisition, a hire, a contract, a tool.
- **How to score:** Rate the gap between what the thing costs and what it is worth. A great thing at a bad price scores low.
- **Quote:** "It has been demonstrated time and time again that no asset is so good that it can’t become a bad investment if bought at too high a price." (S032)
- **Sources:** 5 (S032, S099, S119, S158, S161)

### 4. Margin of safety and defensive investing
- **What it is:** Price should be low enough that you do well, or at least avoid a loss, even if things go wrong. Defense means a solid, identifiable value at a bargain price, spread across holdings, with no reliance on macro forecasts.
- **Parts:** Do not pay prices that presuppose things going right. Make fewer bets that depend on knowing the future (S032, S083, S141).
- **When to use:** Choosing among options by how they behave in a bad year, not only a good one.
- **How to score:** Rate how each option performs if the main assumption is wrong. An option that needs everything to go right scores low.
- **Quote:** "Instead, prices should be so low that you can profit – or at least avoid loss – even if things go wrong." (S032)
- **Sources:** 21 (S032, S036, S051, S053, S057, S066, S070, S073, S081, S083, S095, S096, and 9 more)

## Risk

### 5. Risk is the chance of permanent loss, not volatility
- **What it is:** Investors do not fear fluctuation; they fear a loss they cannot recover from. Fluctuation is temporary. A forced sale at the bottom turns it into a permanent loss.
- **Parts:** Ask what could cause a permanent loss. Check for forced selling: leverage, redemptions, a need for cash (S020, S094, S098).
- **When to use:** Any bet where a bad stretch could force a stop or a sale.
- **How to score:** Rate how likely a bad stretch is to become unrecoverable. Anything that forces a sale at the worst time scores low.
- **Quote:** "What they fear is the possibility of permanent loss." (S098)
- **Sources:** 10 (S020, S026, S066, S094, S095, S098, S102, S135, S148, S159)

### 6. Risk means more things can happen than will happen
- **What it is:** Only one thing actually happens, so the past hides risk. The future is a range of outcomes. People who rely on one forecast think they can remove risk by getting it right.
- **Parts:** Think in ranges and probabilities. Give weight to the bad tail. Doubt that risk can be measured by a single number (S056, S095, S098).
- **When to use:** Reading a plan with a single forecast.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the plan has a range of outcomes and a view of the downside tail. A single-forecast plan scores low.
- **Quote:** "People who rely heavily on forecasts seem to think there’s only one possibility, meaning risk can be eliminated if they just figure out which one it is." (S098)
- **Sources:** 8 (S056, S069, S070, S071, S073, S093, S095, S098)

### 7. Risk control, not risk avoidance
- **What it is:** Return comes from bearing risk, so avoiding risk avoids return. The skill is to take risk where you are paid for it and to control it. Insist on an adequate margin of safety.
- **Parts:** Take risks on purpose. Know the price of each. Stay able to survive when things go badly (S141, S148).
- **When to use:** Deciding how much risk to accept for a given upside.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the risk taken is paid for and survivable.
- **Quote:** "Insist on an adequate margin of safety, or the ability to weather periods when things go less well than you expected." (S141)
- **Sources:** 21 (S027, S044, S046, S048, S049, S050, S055, S074, S076, S078, S079, S083, and 9 more)

### 8. Leverage is neither good nor bad, but it is dangerous at the wrong time
- **What it is:** Debt used at the right time in small amounts to buy cheap assets can help. Debt used late and in large amounts makes a downturn fatal and forces selling.
- **Parts:** When it is used. How much. What it buys (S070, S149).
- **When to use:** Any plan that adds debt or a fixed obligation.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the business survives a bad year with the debt in place.
- **Quote:** "Like just about everything else in investing, leverage is neither good nor bad per se." (S070)
- **Sources:** 93 (S004, S006, S007, S011, S012, S014, S016, S017, S019, S021, S024, S032, and 81 more)

## Cycles and psychology

### 9. You can't predict, but you can prepare
- **What it is:** Forecasts of the economy and the markets do not add value. Cycles are certain, though their timing is not. So prepare for them rather than trying to predict them.
- **Parts:** Dismiss forecasts. Respect cycles. Prepare by controlling risk and keeping the ability to act when others cannot (S024, S037, S133).
- **When to use:** When a plan leans on a view of the economy or the market.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the plan survives either direction of the cycle. A plan that needs a prediction to be right scores low.
- **Quote:** "I continue to believe that cycles are inevitable, often profound, and the most reliable feature of the business and investment worlds." (S037)
- **Sources:** 16 (S019, S024, S026, S031, S037, S043, S053, S055, S063, S064, S076, S124, and 4 more)

### 10. The pendulum of investor psychology
- **What it is:** Sentiment swings between extremes: optimism and pessimism, greed and fear, risk tolerance and risk aversion. Prices follow. The pendulum spends little time at the middle and often goes too far.
- **Parts:** Read where the pendulum is. When others are at an extreme, lean the other way with care (S070, S091).
- **When to use:** Judging how hot or cold a market, a hiring pool, or a sector feels.
- **How to score:** Rate how extreme sentiment looks. Euphoria raises the bar and lowers the score for new money; despair raises it.
- **Quote:** "I probably write more about the pendulum of investor psychology than I do anything else." (S091)
- **Sources:** 35 (S002, S007, S012, S024, S031, S037, S053, S054, S055, S060, S065, S067, and 23 more)

### 11. Contrarianism with a reason
- **What it is:** The best opportunities come when people are eager to sell and the price is low, and the worst when they are bidding hardest. Doing the opposite of the crowd is the most dependable edge, but only when price and value support it.
- **Parts:** Sell what people bid for hardest and buy what they are eager to dump, only when the numbers support it (S032, S097).
- **When to use:** After a move to an extreme.
- **How to score:** Rate how much of the case is the crowd's mood and how much is the price against value.
- **Quote:** "The most important thing is contrarian behavior." (S032)
- **Sources:** 37 (S003, S005, S013, S019, S020, S024, S026, S029, S031, S032, S036, S037, and 25 more)

### 12. What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end
- **What it is:** A good practice or innovation moves from exotic to mainstream to overdone. The early adopters did it safely; the late ones copy it with too much leverage or at too high a price.
- **Parts:** Ask when in the cycle of adoption this sits. Ask what the late arrivals are doing differently (S006, S026, S067, S113).
- **When to use:** Any trend, tool, or tactic the founder sees everywhere.
- **How to score:** Rate how late the move is in its adoption.
- **Quote:** "What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end." (S006)
- **Sources:** 16 (S006, S011, S012, S020, S024, S026, S038, S042, S043, S048, S067, S075, and 4 more)

### 13. Bubbles come from excess optimism applied to a real development
- **What it is:** A bubble is not caused by the technology or the financial innovation itself. It is caused by optimism applied to it past what the facts can support. Bubbles can be useful for building new things, but they punish those who pay the price.
- **Parts:** A real development. Excess optimism. Prices that assume success forever (S019, S154, S161).
- **When to use:** A hot market, such as the AI buildout he writes about.
- **How to score:** Rate whether prices already assume success.
- **Quote:** "Market bubbles aren’t caused directly by technological or financial developments. Rather, they result from the application of excessive optimism to those developments." (S161)
- **Sources:** 78 (S016, S017, S018, S019, S020, S024, S025, S026, S027, S028, S030, S034, and 66 more)

## Timing and humility

### 14. Being too far ahead of your time is indistinguishable from being wrong
- **What it is:** Being right about direction but early looks the same as being wrong, and you may be forced out before you are proved right. So the timing of a contrarian move matters, and so does staying power.
- **Parts:** Expect to look wrong for a while. Do not overstay or move early with money you cannot keep in (S004, S052, S070, S094).
- **When to use:** Betting against a trend or holding through a bad stretch.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the plan can survive being early.
- **Quote:** "There’s always a period – sometimes a long one – when those who follow the crowd look smart and the abstainers look dumb." (S052)
- **Sources:** 11 (S004, S015, S024, S052, S055, S061, S070, S093, S094, S139, S154)

### 15. The "I don't know" school
- **What it is:** No one knows what will happen, and confident opinions about the near term are a mistake. Do not feel certain. Rely on value, survival, and staying power.
- **Parts:** Admit what cannot be known. Build a position that does not depend on knowing it (S068, S124, S140).
- **When to use:** Any plan that requires a forecast to be right.
- **How to score:** Rate how much of the plan's success depends on foreknowledge.
- **Quote:** "No one should feel certain they know what’s going to unfold, or when." (S068)
- **Sources:** 59 (S011, S016, S019, S023, S024, S026, S027, S029, S030, S032, S036, S041, and 47 more)

### 16. Luck versus skill
- **What it is:** Short-run success often comes from being in the right place at the right time. Someone who made an improbable bet and won looks like a genius, but it may be luck and boldness, not skill.
- **Parts:** Ask whether the result was luck. Look at the process, not just the outcome. Be careful with survivorship (S029, S036, S093).
- **When to use:** Judging a track record, a success story, or the founder's own past wins.
- **How to score:** Rate whether the evidence for skill is a repeatable process or just outcomes.
- **Quote:** "But we should recognize that it happened because of luck and boldness, not skill." (S029)
- **Sources:** 12 (S029, S032, S036, S044, S045, S050, S093, S100, S109, S128, S138, S159)
