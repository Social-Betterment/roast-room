# Principles: Howard Marks

18 decision rules, clustered across sources. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Quotes are verbatim from the memos. Where he repeats a line from someone else, the entry says so.

## Think differently

**1. To do better than average, your thinking must be different and better.**
- "In order to outperform, your thinking has to be different and better." (S139)
- "Different and better: that’s a pretty good description of second-level thinking." (S099)
- Sources: 7 (S099, S100, S117, S128, S139, S144, S160). Confidence: high.

**2. Obvious views are already in the price.**
- "First-level thinking is simplistic and superficial, and just about everyone can do it" (S139)
- Sources: 5 (S099, S100, S117, S139, S141). Confidence: high.

**3. No tactic works every time, so beware simple rules.**
- "Among the many things that keep investing from being easy is the fact that no tactic works every time." (S099)
- Sources: 6 (S007, S038, S050, S066, S094, S099). Confidence: high.

## Price and value

**4. The price you pay decides whether a good thing is a good investment.**
- "Bottom line: there’s no such thing as a good idea regardless of price!" (S032)
- "And there are few assets so bad that they can’t be a good investment when bought cheap enough." (S032)
- Sources: 6 (S032, S052, S075, S099, S119, S158). Confidence: high.

**5. Demand a margin of safety.**
- "Instead, prices should be so low that you can profit – or at least avoid loss – even if things go wrong." (S032)
- "Insist on an adequate margin of safety, or the ability to weather periods when things go less well than you expected." (S141)
- Sources: 21 (S032, S036, S051, S053, S057, S066, S070, S073, S081, S083, S095, S096, and 9 more). Confidence: high.

## Risk

**6. Risk is the possibility of permanent loss, not volatility.**
- "What they fear is the possibility of permanent loss." (S098)
- "Permanent loss is very different from volatility or fluctuation." (S098)
- Sources: 10 (S020, S026, S066, S094, S095, S098, S102, S135, S148, S159). Confidence: high.

**7. Risk exists only in the future, and you cannot know the future.**
- "Risk exists only in the future, and it’s impossible to know for sure what the future holds." (S056)
- "No ambiguity is evident when we view the past. Only the things that happened happened." (S095)
- Sources: 3 (S056, S095, S098). Confidence: high.

**8. Do not hold your portfolio hostage to a single forecast.**
- "They try to make fewer investments whose success is heavily dependent on knowing what the future holds, thereby creating an increased margin of safety." (S083)
- Sources: 1 (S083). Confidence: low.

**9. Leverage is dangerous when used late and heavily.**
- "Used at the right time, in judicious amounts, to purchase low-priced assets, it’s a good thing." (S070)
- Sources: 93 (S004, S006, S007, S011, S012, S014, S016, S017, S019, S021, S024, S032, and 81 more). Confidence: high.

## Cycles and psychology

**10. Cycles always prevail. Prepare; do not predict.**
- "Cycles always prevail eventually. Nothing goes in one direction forever. Trees don't grow to the sky. Few things go to zero." (S026)
- "Those who have been readers of my memos for any meaningful period of time know there are a few things I dismiss and a few I believe in thoroughly." (S024)
- Sources: 16 (S019, S024, S026, S031, S037, S043, S053, S055, S063, S064, S076, S124, and 4 more). Confidence: high.

**11. Investor psychology swings like a pendulum, and it swings too far.**
- "I probably write more about the pendulum of investor psychology than I do anything else." (S091)
- "The way I see it, investors face two main risks: the risk of losing money and the risk of missing out." (S070)
- Sources: 35 (S002, S007, S012, S024, S031, S037, S053, S054, S055, S060, S065, S067, and 23 more). Confidence: high.

**12. The late arrivals copy what worked for the early ones, and overdo it.**
- "What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end." (S006)
- "Practices and innovations often move from exotic to mainstream to overdone, especially if they’re initially successful." (S067)
- Sources: 16 (S006, S011, S012, S020, S024, S026, S038, S042, S043, S048, S067, S075, and 4 more). Confidence: high.

**13. Bubbles are excess optimism applied to a real development.**
- "Market bubbles aren’t caused directly by technological or financial developments. Rather, they result from the application of excessive optimism to those developments." (S161)
- "Investors were willing to pay prices that assumed success forever." (S019)
- Sources: 78 (S016, S017, S018, S019, S020, S024, S025, S026, S027, S028, S030, S034, and 66 more). Confidence: high.

**14. The path to superior results runs through other people's mistakes.**
- "the path to superior results lies in taking advantage of other people’s mistakes." (S041)
- "Investors truly do make the same mistakes over and over." (S041)
- Sources: 1 (S041). Confidence: low.

## Timing and humility

**15. Being early looks like being wrong. Plan to survive it.**
- "There’s always a period – sometimes a long one – when those who follow the crowd look smart and the abstainers look dumb." (S052)
- "But the roles are inevitably reversed in the long run." (S052)
- Sources: 11 (S004, S015, S024, S052, S055, S061, S070, S093, S094, S139, S154). Confidence: high.

**16. Nobody knows. Do not hold confident opinions about the near term.**
- "No one should feel certain they know what’s going to unfold, or when." (S068)
- "I think it’s a mistake to hold confident opinions about the events of today." (S068)
- Sources: 59 (S011, S016, S019, S023, S024, S026, S027, S029, S030, S032, S036, S041, and 47 more). Confidence: high.

**17. Stop trying to predict the macro. Study the details.**
- "Stop trying to predict the macro; study the micro like mad in order to know your subject better than others." (S141)
- Sources: 23 (S010, S026, S036, S040, S086, S088, S092, S093, S094, S096, S104, S107, and 11 more). Confidence: high.
